# Reading the registry

Hand-written, and **not** regenerated by `scripts/score_decklists_claude.R`.
Numbers below are from the 2026-08-30 run **after** the `/align-decision-tree`
pass, 1,000 replicates a cell, eight decklists, three cells.

## The first thing to say is what the run cannot tell you

At 1,000 replicates the standard error on one rate near 75% is **±1.4 points**,
and on a difference between two decklists **±2.0**. So a 95% interval on a gap is
roughly **±4 points**, and the run resolves the field into three tiers rather than
into eight positions:

| Tier | Decklists | Going second |
|---|---|---|
| **Top, and not separated** | 5, 7, 8 | 79.5% – 80.5% |
| **Behind, and only just separated** | 2, 4 | 77.8% |
| **Behind that** | 3, 6 | 75.8% – 75.9% |
| **Clearly worst** | 1 | 62.1% |

**The alignment pass sharpened this.** Before it, five lists sat inside a
1.5-point band; now **three** do — decklist5, decklist7 and decklist8 — and they
are the same three in all three cells. decklist2 and decklist4 have fallen a
clear 1.7 points behind them going second and 2.6 going first, which is at the
edge of resolvable and no longer inside the noise.

**The three in the top tier are still a tie with each other.** decklist5 and
decklist7 are level going second at 80.5%, and decklist5 leads going first by
1.3. Separating them is exactly what raising the replicate count is for, and it
is the single most useful thing to do next.

The tiering is **stable across all three cells**, which is worth more than any
individual ordering: the same three lead going second, going first and under the
Item lock, and decklist1 is last in all three by a margin that grows as the cell
gets harder — 18.4 points behind the leader going second, 15.8 going first, and
**20.5** under the lock.

## What separates the tiers, and it is one card

**decklist1 is the only list with no Telepathic Psychic Energy**, running four
basic Psychic Energy instead. That is the whole gap, and the mechanism is visible
in its unmet counts rather than inferred:

| decklist | `[P]` base | unmet A | unmet D |
|---|---|---|---|
| decklist1 | 4 basic Psychic | **102** | 320 |
| decklist2 | 4 Telepathic | 43 | 201 |
| decklist5 | 4 Telepathic + 2 basic | 16 | **173** |
| decklist7 | 4 Telepathic | **8** | 188 |

**Sub-goal A fails 102 times without the Telepathic and 43 times with it**, on
lists that both run two Bronzor. A basic Psychic Energy pays sub-goal D and does
nothing else; a Telepathic pays D *and* searches two Basic `[P]` Pokémon onto the
Bench, which is sub-goal A's cheapest out and the only out that costs no card at
all. The energy base is not a D question. It is an A question wearing a D costume.

**And more `[P]` sources genuinely lowers D.** decklist5 runs six (4 Telepathic +
2 basic) and has the lowest D-unmet in the field at 173; decklist8 runs four and
has 198. That is the one place in the registry where the obvious lever works the
obvious way.

**decklist1 is the one list the alignment pass made *worse*** — 63.8% to 62.1%
going second, and 46.0% to 45.1% under the lock. The cause is the one alignment
fix that costs points: protecting one Duskull and one Rare Candy from the Ultra
Ball discard leaves fewer spare cards, and **decklist1 leans on Ultra Ball harder
than any other list** because it has no Telepathic search to find bodies for
free. The fix stays — it is what the playbook's "surplus … beyond 1" says — and
the effect is one more piece of evidence for the same conclusion: that list's
energy base is what is wrong with it.

## What Salvatore is and is not worth

decklist1 and decklist6 are the two lists with **no Salvatore**, and they are the
only two with a **turn-1 kill count of zero**. Everything else lands between 29
and 41 turn-1 kills in 1,000 games.

**But that speed buys almost no rate**, which is the primary outcome working as
designed (ADR 0004): a turn-1 kill and a turn-2 conventional line count the same.
decklist6 at 75.8% sits 2 points behind decklist2 at 77.8%, and decklist3 — which
*does* run a Salvatore — sits at 75.9% too. So the 2 points are not Salvatore.

**Salvatore's real value is a hedge the metric does not price**: roughly 3% of
games it converts into a turn-1 attack, and those are the games where the
opponent's turn 2 never happens. If that matters, it has to be argued from the
turn-1 column rather than from the hit rate.

## decklist7 and decklist8, read against each other rather than the field

They are a different deck, and three of their cards are worth naming:

- **Sub-goal A is nearly solved in both** — 8 and 6 unmet against 43 for
  decklist2. Three Bronzor, four Poké Pad and two Latias ex is a lot of access.
- **And sub-goal D is not better than the field's** despite that, at 188 and 198.
  They spend their gains on A and hand them back on D, which is the same trade
  the tier table shows as a wash.
- **Both carry cards the window cannot use.** Blissey ex cannot be put into play
  at all — it evolves from Chansey and neither list runs one. Risky Ruins is never
  played, because it damages our own Bench and nobody else's. **That is four of
  sixty cards in decklist7 doing nothing**, and the lists still finish in the top
  tier, which says something about how much slack the deck has.
- **decklist8's number is a lower bound.** Three of its cards are transcribed and
  unimplemented (`docs/03a_card_playbook.md` → *Specified and not implemented*):
  Dunsparce's Trading Places, Dudunsparce's Run Away Draw, and basic Darkness
  Energy as Run Around's cheapest payment. Each of those can only help it.

## Sub-goal D is the largest unmet count in every cell of every list

Twenty-four cells, and D is the biggest number in all twenty-four. §1 of the
decision tree claims **C** is the sub-goal that actually fails, and DT-01 has been
open on that since the first run. This registry does not settle it — D can only be
*unmet* once B and C are met, so it inherits their failures — but the claim has
now survived the rate rising twenty-five points without C and D changing places,
and it is worth deciding rather than leaving open. **S-38 poses it as a position.**
